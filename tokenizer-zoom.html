How Byte-Pair Encoding Works

1. The Vocabulary Problem

If an LLM uses characters, sequences are too long. If it uses whole words, it can't handle typos, new slang, or suffixes (causing massive "Out of Vocabulary" errors). BPE is the mathematical middle-ground.

2. The Training Process

The tokenizer starts by breaking a massive text corpus into raw bytes. It scans for the most frequently adjacent pairs (like 'e' and 'r' -> 'er') and merges them. It repeats this merging process exactly 50,304 times.

3. Handling Spaces (The Ġ Symbol)

Modern tokenizers don't ignore spaces. They treat a space as part of the word to preserve formatting. The symbol Ġ is visually used in the tokenizer dictionary to represent a leading space before a word.

4. Inference (Splitting)

When you input a complex word like "Counterproductive", the tokenizer checks its dictionary. It doesn't have the full word, so it breaks it down into the largest learned subwords it knows: ĠCounter + product + ive.
